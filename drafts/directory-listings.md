## 150-word description

Samurai Vengeance is a side-on action platformer about one samurai fighting through 1000 stages of moonlit rooftops and floating platforms. It is free, runs in any modern browser on desktop or mobile, and needs no download or account.

The combat is built around timing. A three-hit combo builds a damage multiplier. A well-timed parry cancels an attack and turns your next swing into a double-damage finisher. A dash lets you slip straight through danger. Land hits to fill the Ki bar, then spend it on a strike that hits everything on screen.

Every tenth stage is a boss, and each of the ten bosses gets faster and more aggressive below half health. Shield-bearers, archers and diving tengu each need a different answer. Stage modifiers change one rule at a time. There is a daily boss, daily challenges and an interactive tutorial for first-time players.

Progress saves on your device. Signing in syncs it across devices.

## Features

- 1000 stages, with a boss every tenth stage
- Ten named bosses with a faster, more aggressive Phase II at half health
- Three-hit sword combo, a dash with invincibility, and a parry that sets up a double-damage counter
- Ki bar that fills as you land hits and powers a screen-clearing strike
- Four enemy types: grunts, archers, shield-bearers and diving tengu
- Stage modifiers that change one rule, such as Glass Blade (double damage taken, double coins)
- Daily boss and daily challenges
- Interactive tutorial for first-time players
- Three difficulties: Peace, Warrior and Demon
- Five Dojo upgrades bought with coins earned in play
- Original soundtrack generated live in the browser
- Keyboard or touch controls, portrait or landscape
- Saves on the device with no account; optional sign-in syncs progress and adds a leaderboard

## Controls (for sites that ask)

Keyboard: arrow keys or A and D to move, Up or W to jump (tap twice to double jump), Space or Z to attack, Shift or L to dash, hold Q or E to parry, X for the Ki special, hold S or Down to drop through a platform, Escape to pause.

Touch: on-screen buttons for jump, attack, dash, parry and the Ki special.

## IndieDB and Game Jolt: can a game hosted elsewhere be listed?

I could not open either site directly from this environment (both were blocked by the network proxy), so everything here comes from search results and third-party pages. Confirm on each site before submitting.

### IndieDB

- What I could verify: IndieDB's how-to page says you can add your own game yourself, have a team member add it, or add it through a development group. Download hosting is listed as one feature among others (galleries, video, forums), not as a requirement. Source: https://www.indiedb.com/how-to
- New profiles are editable by the creator until staff activate them, so there is a review step before a listing goes public. Source: https://www.indiedb.com/news/temp-permission-system (an older announcement; may be out of date)
- Several browser games hosted on their own sites have IndieDB profiles under the Web platform with a link to their homepage, for example https://www.indiedb.com/games/play4row and https://www.indiedb.com/games/who-online-guessing-game. That suggests a link-only profile is accepted, but I found no written policy saying so.
- Not verified: specific requirements for screenshots, description length, or whether link-only profiles get extra scrutiny.

### Game Jolt

- What I could find: Game Jolt game pages are built around packages, with an option to upload a browser build (an HTML5 .zip with index.html at the root) or a downloadable build. Source (third-party guide): https://wiki.gdevelop.io/gdevelop5/publishing/publishing-to-gamejolt-store/
- An older Game Jolt forum answer says you cannot point a game page at your own site to play, but you can upload the HTML, JS and CSS as a .zip, and link to your site from inside the files. Source: https://gamejolt.com/f/can-we-have-html5-games/486?sort=top (old thread; current policy not confirmed)
- A Construct tutorial lists a Game Jolt requirement that a game must not contain links to other versions of the game on other websites. Source: https://www.construct.net/en/tutorials/publishing-construct-game-295 (third-party and dated)
- Conclusion: a link-only listing on Game Jolt looks unlikely to be accepted. Listing there probably means uploading a browser build. Samurai Vengeance would need its atlases, and its sign-in, payments and counter depend on Netlify functions and Firebase, so an uploaded copy would likely lose saves sync and purchases unless adapted. Not verified against Game Jolt's current rules.
