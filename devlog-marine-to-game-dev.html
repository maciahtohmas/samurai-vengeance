I was a Marine machine gunner. Now I make samurai games.

4 December 2026 · Maciah Thomas

I spent four years in the Marine Corps as a machine gunner. I loved it. It made me who I am today. The discipline, the people, the way you learn to keep going when you're tired and nobody's coming to help. I still carry all of that.

When I got out, I had to figure out what came next. I've loved tech my whole life, so I knew it would be something with computers. I wanted to build something that actually did something to the person on the other side, something people would feel. And I wanted a real chance at income that didn't depend on me trading every hour for a paycheck. A game checked every box.

Why samurai

I love martial arts. I've always been drawn to swords and the craft of making them, and The Last Samurai is one of my favorite movies. Samurai Vengeance comes from all of that. The combat is built around timing: you wait for the hit, parry it at the last second, and punish the opening. Mashing buttons gets you killed. Reading your opponent gets you through.

The game has 1,000 stages, a boss every ten, and it runs free in your browser on a phone or a computer. There's also a comic series that goes with it, The Indigo Ronin, about a samurai who loses his lord and his home in one night and goes up the mountain after the men who did it.

How I build it

I come up with the ideas and I vibe code with AI. I decide what the game should feel like, what the next boss does, what's broken, what's missing. AI helps me write the code and make the art. Then I play it, find what feels wrong, and we go again.

Some people don't like that. Get over it. This is the AI revolution, and I'd rather learn how to use the new tools than sit around arguing about them. Every developer before me used whatever tools their time had. These are mine.

The hard part

The hardest part wasn't the code. It was figuring out which AI agents to use and how to get them to build what was in my head. There are a lot of tools out there, and most of them don't do what they promise.

The other hard part is marketing. I'm an introvert. Getting on camera and talking about myself is not my thing. But a game nobody sees is a game nobody plays, so I'm learning that too, one post at a time.

The best day

The best day so far was simple. It was the day the game went live and anyone in the world could play it from a link.

What I hope you see

Samurai Vengeance is a look inside my imagination. It's the stuff I've loved my whole life, samurai and swords and tech, finally built into something real. A year ago it only existed in my head. Now you can play it.

If you try it, I want to hear what stage you reached and which boss got you. The contact page has every way to reach me, and there's more about me on the about the creator page.
